# Live Pod Migration — Safety Invariants (`I1`–`I12`)

The Live Pod Migration (`LPM`) controller evaluates a set of twelve safety invariants (`I1`–`I12`) at the end of every successful reconciliation step across `PodMigrationJobReconciler`, `PodGateReconciler`, and `PodMigrationReconciler`. Each invariant predicate is stateless, but two of them, `I10` and `I11`, are debounced: the controller tracks their consecutive violations in memory (see [Debounced Rules](#debounced-rules)).

Each invariant predicate inspects an in-memory `ReconcileSnapshot` built from the reconciled object and the controller-runtime local informer cache. For `PodMigrationJobReconciler`, the cache also serves these reads, none of which goes to the API server except in the one case described below:

- the reconciled job's `PodSnapshotManualTrigger` and `PodSnapshot`;
- the `PodSnapshot` of each other job in the namespace that is `Snapshotting` with a `Status.SnapshotRef`, past its timeout but still inside the 2-hour hard cap (#199).

`PodGateReconciler` also reads one `PodSnapshot` from the same cache: that of the job attached to the reconciled Pod (its `pod-migration.gke.io/assigned-pmj` annotation), when that job is `Snapshotting` with a `Status.SnapshotRef`, past its timeout but still inside the 2-hour hard cap, so that `I4` sees the job's progress there too (#231).

Only kinds whose CRDs existed when the controller started are watched and read. For a kind without a CRD at startup, the controller skips the read and treats the object as absent, the same result a live read of a missing CRD gives, so a cluster without, for example, the GKE Pod Snapshots CRDs runs normally. Restart the controller after installing a snapshot engine's CRDs.

The exception: when the cache shows a `Succeeded` or `Failed` job's trigger as not yet deleted, the controller makes one live read before reporting `I5`. The reconcile that concluded the job may have deleted the trigger just before the evaluation, and the cache may not have seen the delete yet.

## Execution Modes (`--invariant-mode`)

- `disabled` (default): Disables invariant evaluation and skips all snapshot/informer reads (`Engine.Enabled() == false`).
- `observe`: Logs structured errors, increments `pod_migration_invariant_violations_total{invariant="I1".."I12"}`, and emits a deduplicated Kubernetes `Warning` Event (`Reason=InvariantViolation`) on first observation without interrupting the active migration. Note: enabling `observe` or `strict` performs one local informer-cache namespace `List` of `Pod`s and `PodMigrationJob`s per `PodMigrationJob` reconcile to evaluate cross-object invariants (every rule except `I5` reads these lists); `PodGateReconciler` and `PodMigrationReconciler` perform zero namespace `List`s.
- `strict`: Performs all `observe` actions. In addition, whenever `PodMigrationJobReconciler` finds a violation of the job it is reconciling, it transitions that job, if it is non-terminal, to `Phase=Failed` (`Reason=InvariantViolation`), cleans up its `PodSnapshotManualTrigger`, and suppresses further requeues; a replacement Pod that is still gated is then released for a clean cold start. A violation is the job's own when it names that job (`Violation.PMJName`) or names no job. The rules scan every job and Pod in the namespace, so the snapshot can also show violations of other jobs, including terminal ones; those are logged, counted, and evented as in `observe` mode, but they never fail the reconciled job (#198). Each job fails only for its own violations, when it is reconciled itself. The failed job's `Ready` message names one of its own violations. `PodGateReconciler` and `PodMigrationReconciler` discard the strict-mode result, so violations seen only there never fail a job. The failure happens immediately for every rule except the debounced `I10` and `I11`: their violations are reported at once, but the job fails only after the debounce threshold is reached (see [Debounced Rules](#debounced-rules)).

## Debounced Rules

`I10` and `I11` are wrapped in `NewDebouncedRule(..., 3, 30*time.Second)` because both read state that the informer cache can show late: whether the replacement Pod still exists (`I10`), and whether the source Pod already carries its fence label and `Ready=False` (`I11`). Every other rule is immediate. The predicates themselves stay stateless; the engine keeps the debounce state in memory, separately for each reconciler, reconciled object (by UID), and rule. The one exception is `PodGateReconciler`: for a Pod with a job attached (the job named by its `pod-migration.gke.io/assigned-pmj` annotation), it keys the state by that `PodMigrationJob`, so all Pods attached to the same job share one count. In either case only that job's own violations (see `strict` above) are samples for its state; another job's violations are counted only when that job is reconciled, so they never move this job toward a failure (#198). For a Pod with no job attached, every violation counts toward the Pod's state.

- Reporting is not delayed. A newly observed violation is logged, counted in `pod_migration_invariant_violations_total`, and emitted as a `Warning` Event whose message ends with `[sample <n>/3, min 30s]`. A violation of another job is reported in the same way but without the sample suffix, since it is not counted for the reconciled object. Repeats with the same message are suppressed while every sample still reports the violation; after a sample without it, even a degraded one, the next occurrence is reported again.
- Only the `strict`-mode failure is delayed. It happens once the rule has fired on at least 3 consecutive samples (one sample per reconcile) and at least 30 seconds have passed since the first of them. The engine then emits an `escalating to strict abort` Event (once per run of consecutive violations, per reconciler), and `PodMigrationJobReconciler` fails the `PodMigrationJob` it is reconciling. `PodGateReconciler` can emit this Event too, on the job attached to its Pod, although nothing fails there. In `observe` mode, the engine instead logs and emits a `would escalate` Event (also once per run, per reconciler), and the migration continues.
- A healthy sample in which the rule does not fire for the reconciled job resets the count, even if it still fires for another job. A degraded sample, in which the informer `List` of `Pod`s or `PodMigrationJob`s failed (`PodListFailed` / `PMJListFailed`), neither counts nor resets it.
- The state is dropped when the `PodMigrationJob` reaches a terminal phase (the rule then no longer escalates), or when the reconciled object is deleted. Deleting a Pod doesn't drop state that `PodGateReconciler` keyed by an attached job, and neither does deleting that job: `ForgetObject` clears only the deleted object's own key, under the reconciler of that object. Once the engine tracks 4096 reconciler-and-object keys, each new key evicts an arbitrary existing one (in map iteration order, not the oldest). Because the state lives only in memory, a controller restart or leader change starts the count over.

Combined with each predicate's own wait, `strict` mode fails a job at the earliest about 60 seconds after `Status.RestoringStartTime` for `I10` (30 seconds before the predicate fires, plus the 30-second debounce), and about 40 seconds after `SourceFenced` turned `True` for `I11` (the 10-second `SourceFencedGraceWindow`, plus the debounce). If the `SourceFenced` condition has a zero `LastTransitionTime`, the grace window is skipped: `I11` can fire as soon as the job shows `SourceFenced=True`, so `strict` mode can fail the job about 30 seconds after that.

## The Invariants (`I1`–`I12`)

### `I1` — At-Most-Once Restore (`AtMostOnceRestore`)
A `PodSnapshot` represents a point-in-time checkpoint of a single process tree and its file descriptors, sequence numbers, and in-memory state. Restoring the same snapshot into two concurrent Pod instances creates a split-brain state where two processes believe they own the same session or lock. `I1` verifies that no two Pods in the namespace are ordered to restore the same snapshot, and that once a `PodMigrationJob` has marked `Status.Consumed=true` and `Status.GateReleased=true` for a specific `Status.RestoredPodUID`, no Pod with a different UID is ordered to restore that snapshot under an annotation that job's engine reads. That second violation names the job the rebound Pod is attached to (its `pod-migration.gke.io/assigned-pmj` annotation), since that job is the one about to restore the consumed snapshot and is the one `strict` mode fails; it names the consuming job only when the Pod has no job attached.

Each snapshot engine orders a restore with its own Pod annotation, `<api-group>/ps-name`: `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name` for GKE Pod Snapshots, and `<criu-api-group>/ps-name` for CRIU (`criu.podmigration.gke.io/ps-name` by default). Its value is the snapshot name, or `""` for an explicit cold start (the cold-start bypass). `I1` reads these annotations as follows:

- **Which order counts.** To find duplicates, `I1` reads a Pod's order for the engine of the job it is attached to (a job without `pod-migration.gke.io/engine` is a GKE Pod Snapshots job); other engines' annotations on the Pod don't count. A gVisor Pod (`runtimeClassName: gvisor`) also restores the snapshot named in its `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name`, whatever engine serves its job, because GKE Pod Snapshots reads that annotation when it creates the Pod's sandbox (#151). If the Pod has no job attached, or its job isn't among the jobs the rule sees, `I1` uses the first order it finds, checking GKE Pod Snapshots first. The rebind check counts the same orders for a Pod whose job is known, as long as the consumed job's engine reads that annotation or the annotation is unknown; for any other Pod, it reads the Pod's order for the consumed job's engine.
- **Same name, different API groups.** A snapshot belongs to the API group of the annotation that orders it, so `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name: snap-1` on one Pod and `criu.podmigration.gke.io/ps-name: snap-1` on another are two snapshots, not a duplicate. Engines that share a group, such as CRIU installed with `--criu-api-group=podsnapshot.gke.io`, share its snapshots. If `I1` can't tell which annotation an order came from, it treats the order as possibly any group's snapshot.
- **How the rules learn an engine's annotation.** `I1` and `I2` ask the engine registry (`ReconcileSnapshot.RestoreOrders`) which annotation each engine reads. Without a registry, they treat every engine as reading `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name`.

### `I2` — No Silent Cold-Start (`NoSilentColdStart`)
A `PodMigrationJob` must never report `Phase=Succeeded` (which signals a verified warm checkpoint restore) if the replacement Pod actually started cold without restoring from the snapshot. With restore orders read per engine as described under `I1`, `I2` checks three things:

- **Succeeded job.** A `PodMigrationJob` in `Phase=Succeeded` must have a non-empty `Status.SnapshotRef`, and its replacement Pod must carry the restore order of the job's engine set to `Status.SnapshotRef`, rather than that engine's explicit cold-start bypass (for GKE Pod Snapshots, `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name: ""`); another engine's order doesn't count. That replacement check matches by `RestoredPodUID` when recorded, skipping pods replaced under the same name. It accepts the cold-start bypass if another `PodMigrationJob` set the bypass on the pod (stamping `pod-migration.gke.io/cold-start-bypass-job` matching `FormatPMJName`, #163) or a job created no earlier than it, among the jobs the rule sees, targets the replacement's UID (#151); this exemption is strictly guarded by the empty cold-start bypass order so that a replacement carrying a different snapshot name still violates `I2` (#163). It also accepts the cold-start bypass once the job has recorded `Restored=True` (with reason `RestoreVerified` or `RestoreInferred`) and the pod carries the durable `pod-migration.gke.io/restore-completed` marker matching the snapshot: after a verified restore, the controller sets both on the replacement, so that a later restart of its sandbox starts cold instead of restoring the migration snapshot again (#152).
- **Released replacement.** If a gated replacement Pod assigned to a `PodMigrationJob` has its `gke.io/pod-migration-gate` removed, it must carry a restore order for its job's engine, either a snapshot name or the explicit empty-string bypass, so it cannot accidentally restore a stale snapshot. When its job isn't among the jobs the rule sees, any registered engine's order counts. A job that names an engine the controller doesn't have has no order the rule can read, so its released replacement is reported; the controller fails that job with `EngineNotRegistered` if it hasn't finished.
- **gVisor replacement served by another engine.** A released gVisor replacement whose job's engine doesn't read `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name` must also carry `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name: ""` (reason `GVisorPodMissingGKEColdStartBypass`). GKE Pod Snapshots reads that annotation when it creates the sandbox of every gVisor Pod, and without the bypass it can restore a leftover snapshot into it (#151). This check skips Pods that are terminating or still gated, Pods of other runtime classes, Pods whose job isn't among the jobs the rule sees, and jobs whose engine itself reads `podsnapshot.gke.io/ps-name` (GKE Pod Snapshots jobs, single-group installs, and evaluations without a registry).

### `I3` — Gate Liveness (`GateLiveness`)
Replacement Pods opted into migration receive the `gke.io/pod-migration-gate` scheduling gate at admission so they remain `Pending` (`SchedulingGated`) until checkpointing and volume detachment finish. If a `PodMigrationJob` reaches a terminal phase (`Succeeded`, `Failed`, or `SucceededWithoutRestore`) and is consumed, or if it has already set `Status.GateReleased=true` for the replacement Pod's UID, leaving the scheduling gate on the Pod permanently strands the workload unschedulable. `I3` verifies that no non-terminating Pod retains `gke.io/pod-migration-gate` after its assigned `PodMigrationJob` is terminal and consumed, or beyond the 30-second two-step release grace window (`GateReleaseGraceWindow`) after `Status.GateReleased=true`.

### `I4` — Terminal Progress (`TerminalProgress`)
Every active migration (`Pending`, `Snapshotting`, `Evicting`) and every `PodMigration` deletion deferral (`podmigration.gke.io/storage-cleanup` finalizer) must terminate within a bounded deadline rather than hanging indefinitely. `I4` reads the effective migration timeout stamped on `pod-migration.gke.io/timeout` (capped at 2 hours and falling back to 10 minutes) plus a 30-second reconciliation slack window, runs that deadline and its 2-hour hard cap (`MaxMigrationTimeout`) from the job's creation while it is `Pending` and from `Status.SnapshottingStartTime` once it has left `Pending` (so a wait for the PodDisruptionBudget in `Pending` doesn't use up the migration's own time, #42), tolerates `Snapshotting` jobs with a non-empty `Status.SnapshotRef` while the snapshot reports forward progress (`LastProgressTime` within the effective timeout plus 30-second slack, excluding failed and refused snapshots, snapshots being deleted as the reconciler reports them (#265), and stalled reasons such as `CheckpointTimedOut` and `CancelRequested`) up to the 2-hour hard cap (#222), anchors `Evicting` phase progress on `Status.EvictingStartTime` (or `pod-migration.gke.io/evicting-since` fallback) within that same 2-hour hard cap, and verifies that no active `PodMigrationJob` or deleting `PodMigration` exceeds its deadline without transitioning to a terminal state. For a `Ready` snapshot, `LastProgressTime` is when it became `Ready`, if the snapshot provider reports that time (#231). The reconciler moves a job to `Evicting` as soon as it sees its snapshot `Ready`, so once a job is past its deadline, `I4` tolerates it in `Snapshotting` for at most the effective timeout plus 30 seconds after its snapshot became `Ready`. A `Ready` snapshot whose provider doesn't report when it became `Ready` is tolerated up to the hard cap.

### `I5` — Zero Resource Leak (`ZeroResourceLeak`)
When a `PodMigrationJob` concludes in `Phase=Succeeded` or `Phase=Failed`, the temporary `PodSnapshotManualTrigger` (`psmt-<pod>-<uid>`) created to trigger the GKE checkpoint must be deleted so stale triggers do not accumulate in etcd or block subsequent migrations (`Phase=SucceededWithoutRestore` intentionally preserves trigger/snapshot state for diagnostics per `#32`). `I5` inspects the local cache whenever a `PodMigrationJob` is in `Succeeded` or `Failed` and flags the job's un-deleted `PodSnapshotManualTrigger` (found by name in the job's engine API group). Before it reports, one live read checks that the trigger is still there and not being deleted, because the cache can miss a delete made moments earlier. If that read fails for another reason, the cached answer is used.

### `I6` — Revision & Identity Fidelity (`RevisionAndIdentityFidelity`)
During rolling updates (`Deployment` or `StatefulSet` rollout) or `Indexed Job` execution, a checkpoint taken from an older revision or different completion index must never be restored into a replacement Pod belonging to a newer revision or different index. `I6` verifies that when a `PodMigrationJob` is bound to a replacement Pod (`Status.RestoredPodName`), their `pod-template-hash` (`pod-migration.gke.io/pod-template-hash`), `controller-revision-hash`, `batch.kubernetes.io/job-completion-index`, and parent workload UID (`pod-migration.gke.io/parent-uid`) match.

### `I7` — Disruption Bounding & PDB Compliance (`DisruptionBoundingPDBCompliance`)
Terminating the origin Pod during `Phase=Evicting` must respect Kubernetes `PodDisruptionBudget` (`PDB`) constraints via the `policy/v1` `Eviction` subresource rather than issuing a raw `Delete` before the migration deadline expires. The one exception is a job the node preemption controller created (`pod-migration.gke.io/trigger-source: spot-preemption`, for Spot preemption or GKE host maintenance): the node goes away whatever the budget says, so it deletes the source directly, with its UID as a precondition and a 1 s grace period (#305). `I7` verifies that a `PodMigrationJob` never advances to `Restoring` or `Succeeded` while `BlockedByPDB=True` / `EvictionMisconfigured=True` remains active or while the non-terminating origin Pod (`TargetPodUID`) is still running.

### `I8` — Platform & Control-Plane Isolation (`PlatformAndControlPlaneIsolation`)
System and control-plane components running in `kube-system`, `pod-migration-system`, or `gke-managed-*` namespaces must never be intercepted, gated, or checkpointed by the migration controller, as gating critical cluster infrastructure (including the migration webhook/controller itself) can deadlock the cluster. `I8` verifies that no Pod in an excluded system namespace carries `gke.io/pod-migration-gate` and no `PodMigrationJob` is created in those namespaces.

### `I9` — Deterministic Fallback over Crashloop (`DeterministicFallbackOverCrashloop`)
When the container runtime (`gVisor` / `runsc` or `runc`) fails to restore a container from a snapshot (surfacing as `StartError` / `RunContainerError` with exit code `128` and a known engine restore-crash signature classified by `restore.Classify`), the controller must deterministically fail the `PodMigrationJob`, delete the crashed replacement Pod, and allow a clean cold start rather than leaving the Pod in `CrashLoopBackOff` or marking the migration `Succeeded`. `I9` verifies via live `restore.Classify` inspection and `RestoreCrashed` conditions that no `PodMigrationJob` with a fatal restore crash remains in `Restoring` or transitions to `Succeeded`.

### `I10` — Restoring Replacement Liveness (`RestoringReplacementLiveness`)
When a `PodMigrationJob` has remained in `Phase=Restoring` for at least 30 seconds with a bound `Status.RestoredPodName` (and informer pod listing is healthy), that replacement Pod must still exist and be non-deleting in the namespace; otherwise, the `PodMigrationJob` is wedged waiting on a missing replacement Pod. Evaluated via a 3-sample / 30-second debounced wrapper (`NewDebouncedRule`) to tolerate transient informer cache lag. `I10` only fires during `PodMigrationJobReconciler` reconciles and reports reason `RestoringReplacementPodMissing`. Because the predicate itself waits 30 seconds after `Status.RestoringStartTime` and the debounce then needs another 30 seconds, `strict` mode fails the job at the earliest about 60 seconds after `Status.RestoringStartTime` (see [Debounced Rules](#debounced-rules)).

### `I11` — Source Fenced After Checkpoint (`SourceFencedAfterCheckpoint`)
Once the controller fences an origin Pod for migration and records `SourceFenced=True` on an active `PodMigrationJob` (`#131`, `#154`), the origin Pod must remain excluded from `Service` `EndpointSlice`s and `PodDisruptionBudget` healthy counts for as long as the migration remains active and fenced. `I11` verifies (after a 10-second `SourceFencedGraceWindow` off `SourceFenced.LastTransitionTime` and wrapped in a 3-sample / 30-second `NewDebouncedRule` to tolerate informer cache lag) that whenever a non-terminal `PodMigrationJob` has condition `SourceFenced=True`, its origin Pod (matched by `TargetPodUID` / `PodRef.Name`), while it exists, carries the label `pod-migration.gke.io/fenced: "true"` and has pod status condition `Ready=False` with `Reason=PodMigrationFenced`. When unfencing a source Pod on a pre-checkpoint abort or cancel path, the controller must set `SourceFenced=False` (`Reason=Unfenced`) on the `PodMigrationJob` before removing `pod-migration.gke.io/fenced` from the Pod; terminal `PodMigrationJob`s (`Succeeded`, `SucceededWithoutRestore`, `Failed`) are skipped so an unfenced Pod from a concluded job never flags active migrations in the namespace.

### `I12` — Restore Outcome Confirmed (`RestoreOutcomeConfirmed`)
A `PodMigrationJob` may record `Reason=RestoreVerified` on its `Restored` or `Ready` condition only when its snapshot engine positively confirmed the restore (`RestoreVerdict{Outcome: Restored, Confirmed: true}`) with non-empty confirmation evidence recorded in the `Restored` condition message (`#79`, `#179`, C6). Because GKE Pod Snapshots exposes no positive restore signal in GKE today, a job served by `gke-pod-snapshots` must report `Reason=RestoreInferred` (never `RestoreVerified`). In addition, `I12` verifies as an immediate (non-debounced) rule that no non-`Succeeded` `PodMigrationJob` carries `Restored=True`, and that any `Succeeded` `PodMigrationJob` with a `Restored` condition sets `Restored=True` with `Reason=RestoreVerified` or `Reason=RestoreInferred` matching `Ready`.
